# 2026-11-12 — Ridderstad 2010 appendix count audit

## Question

Can a rights-clear, scholarly transcription source clarify the project's 241-versus-242 sign-impression discrepancy, or provide a published per-ring tally?

## Source and access tier

Marianna P. Ridderstad, “Lunisolar Calendrical Symbolism on the Phaistos Disk,” *Anistoriton* 12, In Situ 2 (2010), 5 pp. The University of Helsinki Research Portal classifies it as a peer-reviewed, A1 refereed journal article:

- Institutional record: https://researchportal.helsinki.fi/en/publications/lunisolar-calendrical-symbolism-on-the-phaistos-disk/
- Publisher PDF link listed by the portal: http://www.anistor.gr/english/enback/2010_2s_Anistoriton.pdf

The Helsinki record verifies the article's bibliographic and peer-review metadata, not the preview's fidelity. Treat the transcription reading as a scholarly-source reading pending direct PDF verification.

## Reproducible count check

The article's prose states **241 signs**. Its Appendix 2 prints numerical sequences for A1–A31 and B1–B30. Counting the hyphen-delimited positions in those printed sequences gives:

- Side A: 31 groups, 123 positions.
- Side B: 30 groups, 119 positions.
- Total: 61 groups, 242 positions.

A8 is printed as `02-12-06-18-?`: the final position is explicitly unreadable/unknown. The 242-position appendix total therefore includes one unknown slot. The article's 241 prose total may count only identifiable impressions while its transcription preserves the damaged position as a slot. This is a plausible explanation for the 241/242 split, **not a confirmed resolution**: the article does not explicitly state that this is its counting convention.

The same appendix provides a full group sequence, but **no per-ring sign-count table**. Its calendrical interpretation is the author's hypothesis and is not adopted here.

## Limits and next check

- This is arithmetic over a published transcription, not a new visual recount of the disc.
- It does not settle whether the A8 damaged position is one impression, two overlapping impressions, or a transcription convention.
- It does not resolve Ring 1's divider ambiguity at x=600–1200.
- No image bytes or restricted museum material were acquired.

**Next check:** compare A8 against the pinned CC0 Side A image and the cited Godart (1995:101) discussion before promoting the 241/242 explanation. A per-ring tally still requires an explicit mapping from each numbered group to a physical spiral turn and an independent visual check.


## Independent scholarly cross-check: Reczko (2009) and erratum

I directly read Wolfgang Reczko, “Analyzing and dating the structure of the Phaistos Disk,” *Archaeological and Anthropological Sciences* 1, 241–245 (2009), DOI [10.1007/s12520-009-0015-2](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12520-009-0015-2). The article explicitly states **241 symbols**, selected from **45 unique types**, grouped in **61 boxes**; it describes 31 boxes on Side A and 30 on Side B. This is an independent peer-reviewed publication of the 241 total, not a new count from the disc.

I also read Reczko's linked erratum, DOI [10.1007/s12520-009-0020-5](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12520-009-0020-5), published 20 November 2009. It corrects the article's side-specific box description: the small 18b box is on Side A after the 18th spiral box. The erratum does **not** amend the 241-symbol total. Therefore, Reczko corroborates 241 as a published count convention, but neither the article nor its erratum explains whether the unreadable A8 position was excluded. The proposed identifiable-signs-versus-transcription-slots explanation remains plausible, not confirmed.

The article's own calendrical/Saros interpretation is not adopted. No image recount, new sign reading, or translation was performed in this cross-check.
