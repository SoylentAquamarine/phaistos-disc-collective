# SQ-3 — first pass: does the disc's sign inventory resemble Cretan Hieroglyphic?

## Precommitment self-review (written before research)

**Known:** SQ-3 ("Comparative structural analysis") has no status note at all — entirely
unstarted, unlike every other sidequest in this project. Its own scope explicitly allows a
literature-verification first step independent of SQ-2's full atlas: "Flag any specific claimed
resemblance (several are cited in general background sources) as needing direct verification
against a primary or citable scholarly source before being relied upon."

**Not done yet:** no search for the actual scholarly state of "does the disc resemble Cretan
Hieroglyphic" has been run; no specific claimed resemblance has been checked against a primary
or citable source.

**Design and why it's non-circular:** search for the actual scholarly literature on this
specific comparison (not assuming an answer either direction), then attempt to directly fetch
the most load-bearing sources found, disclosing honestly if access is blocked rather than
silently resting on search-synthesis.

**Honesty precommitment:** report every source's actual tier (direct fetch vs. search-synthesis)
and flag any cross-reference to this project's own existing SQ-4 catalog entries rather than
treating a found connection as coincidental.

## Findings

**Arthur Evans (1921), the earliest systematic treatment**: search-synthesis tier (direct fetch
of the digitized Heidelberg scan was blocked by bot-protection, "Access Denied" from Anubis —
disclosed, not silently dropped). Evans reportedly concluded the disc's characters do **not**
map closely onto the root-forms of the Linear scripts, "except in one or two cases" — one
specific example cited: a disc sign Evans associated with a Linear sign he read as "manacles,"
and the disc's flying-bird sign, which he judged to resemble Cretan bird-sign types generally
but differ specifically in its talons.

**Ferrara, Montecchi & Valério (2022, *Pasiphae*), the more rigorous recent academic source**:
search-synthesis tier (direct fetch of the PDF returned HTTP 403, disclosed). Reported to treat
the Phaistos Disc as "a separate entity" sitting alongside Cretan Hieroglyphic and Linear A,
rather than as a close relative of either — their own analysis concerns how CH and Linear A
relate to *each other* (finding a meaningful fraction of Linear A signs are not shared with CH,
consistent with "substantial adaptation" rather than simple continuity), not a specific
sign-by-sign disc-to-CH comparison.

**A minority, competing claim — and a direct cross-reference to this project's own SQ-4
catalog**: a separate line of work argues the disc's script is closer to **Luwian
Hieroglyphic** than to Cretan Linear scripts or Egyptian, reporting a specific figure: "of the
47 signs, 31 can be associated with a corresponding Luwian hieroglyphic." This is almost
certainly the same claim already in this project's SQ-4 prior-claims catalog —
**Achterberg, Best, Enzler & Rietveld's Luwian-letter-to-Nestor claim** — but the specific
"31 of 47 signs" correspondence figure is new, not yet recorded in that catalog entry. Not
confirmed this cycle whether this is the identical paper or a related/updated figure from the
same research program — flagged as a concrete cross-check for a future cycle, not merged in
without verification.

## Outcome

SQ-3 is no longer entirely untouched — a first-pass literature review now exists, finding the
mainstream view is skeptical of a close CH resemblance (Evans's own early conclusion, echoed by
the more rigorous 2022 study treating the disc as its own separate case), while the one
specific "close resemblance" claim found (Luwian Hieroglyphic, not Cretan Hieroglyphic at all)
already exists in this project's own SQ-4 catalog under a different framing. **Preliminary
answer to SQ-3's own deliverable question** ("does the evidence support pursuing a Cretan-
Hieroglyphic-linked reading further"): no strong support found this pass — the field's own
closest-resemblance claim points toward a different, more distant script (Luwian) instead.

## Limitation disclosed

Every source in this log is search-synthesis tier — both direct-fetch attempts (Evans 1921,
Ferrara et al. 2022) were blocked (bot-protection and HTTP 403 respectively). This is a
first-pass literature orientation, not SQ-3's full sign-by-sign deliverable, which still
requires SQ-2's atlas per the sidequest's own stated scope.
