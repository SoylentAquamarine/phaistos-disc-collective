# Institutional archival lead: Pernier diary and July 1908 letters

**Date checked:** 2026-11-26
**Source:** Nicola Cucuzza, *Il disco di Festòs (1908)*, hosted by the Scuola Archeologica Italiana di Atene: https://scuoladiatene.it/images/documents/Il_disco_di_Festo%CC%80s_1908.pdf

## What the institutional account says

The account summarizes the excavation diary and correspondence written shortly after the disc's discovery. It says:

- Pernier's diary entry was written on 4 July 1908, one day after the discovery.
- The disc was brought to Pernier at about 7 p.m. on 3 July by Zacharias Iliakis, the excavation foreman.
- Pernier appended a note about the discovery to a letter to Luigi Pigorini on 3 July.
- A postcard to Federico Halbherr followed on 4 July; a fuller letter to Halbherr on 6 July included a rubbing and further information.
- The account identifies the Italian Archaeological School of Athens as the repository of Pernier's diary.

The article is an institutional secondary account, not direct access to the diary or letters. It gives a concrete archival route and exact dates for a text-only request, but does not supply an archival call number or reproduction terms. It also repeats a total of 242 impressions; this does not resolve Pernier's reported 241 versus the institutional 242 count.

## Archive access route checked

The SAIA's own [Archives page](https://scuoladiatene.it/biblioteca-e-archivi/archivi.html) states that its archives are public and freely consultable, except copies of documents belonging to the Italian Superintendency of the Dodecanese, which require Greek Ministry authorization. It lists notebooks, excavation reports, travel records, institutional and personal documents, and says open-access digitization began in 2017. The page links a [document-request form](https://scuoladiatene.it/archivio-fotografico-e-documentazione.html).

The form requires the requester's name, email and institution, plus the reason, research topic, document requested and inventory number. No specific Pernier diary/letter inventory number was located in the open-access listings reviewed here. No request was submitted; no archive was contacted. This gives a concrete text-only access route but does not establish that the 1908 diary or letters are already digitized or immediately available. Publication of archival apographs is separately authorized by SAIA.
