# Execution — Ring 1 count attempt: a real, disclosed difficulty found, not a clean tally

Precommitment: `logs/2026-10-13-sq2-ring1-count-selfreview.md` (frozen before this ran).

## What was done

Viewed the now-corrected polar-unwrap image (3600×1600, both named blockers resolved last
cycle) in full-height 600px-wide segments, tracking Ring 1's own row and counting bold, full-
height vertical dividers using the already-validated criterion.

**An initial restricted-height crop attempt (y=0-420 only) was tried first and abandoned**: it
reproduced the same "fixed band" problem the prior cycle's methodology fix was specifically
designed to avoid — Ring 1's own real position drifts enough across the circumference that a
fixed y-range sometimes cut into Ring 1 and sometimes missed part of it. Switched to full-
height segments (no fixed band at all) for the actual attempt, consistent with the validated
method.

## Result: a real difficulty, disclosed rather than forced through

**Segment 0 (x=0-600)**: one clear, bold, full-height divider found, splitting this segment's
Ring 1 content into two legible groups (left: a partial sign, a wheel/dots sign, a crown-like
sign, an arrow-like sign; right: a ladder/shield-pattern sign, a vase-shaped sign, a wheel/dots
sign cut at the segment's right edge).

**Segment 1 (x=600-1200)**: **no clearly identifiable bold divider was found across the entire
600px width of this segment's Ring 1 row**, despite the row containing what looks like at least
4-5 distinct individual signs (a small blob, a thin vertical squiggle, a fish/bird-like shape, a
star/blob shape, a wheel/dots sign, a bottle shape). By contrast, Ring 2 directly below, in the
same segment, shows clearly visible dividers separating cells of 2-3 grouped signs each — a
real, visible contrast in divider legibility between these two adjacent rings in the same image,
same lighting, same crop.

**This is the actual finding, not an incidental snag**: Ring 1's own cell dividers are
markedly less consistently visible than Ring 2's (and, per earlier cycles' direct reads, Ring
3's and Ring 4's) in this photograph. Two honest possibilities, not distinguished by this pass:
(1) Ring 1 is more worn/eroded than the inner rings (plausible — the outermost ring is most
exposed to handling and environmental wear over the object's lifetime), making real dividers
present but harder to see in this specific photograph; or (2) Ring 1's own actual cell
boundaries are genuinely less frequent or differently structured than the inner rings', which
would itself be a real structural fact about the inscription, not a photographic limitation.

## Outcome

**No full Ring 1 tally is reported** — forcing a count through segment 1's genuinely ambiguous
stretch would risk a confidently-wrong number, exactly what this project's own standing
discipline exists to prevent. What this attempt *does* produce: a real, specific, named
difficulty (Ring 1's own divider visibility, not just "the disc is hard to read" in general),
localized to a specific segment (x=600-1200) for anyone who wants to examine it directly, and a
disclosed open question (wear vs. genuine structural difference) worth checking against a
published scholarly transcription or higher-resolution/raking-light photography before
attempting a confident count of this specific ring.

## Decision

The next concrete step is not "try harder to count Ring 1 by eye" — this pass shows that
wouldn't resolve the actual difficulty found. Instead: (a) check whether a published Phaistos
Disc catalog (e.g. the sign-inventory sources already cited elsewhere in this project) reports
per-ring cell counts that could be cross-checked against what *is* clearly countable here, or
(b) attempt Ring 2, 3, or 4 instead, where divider visibility looks more consistent based on
this and prior cycles' direct reads, and come back to Ring 1 once a comparison baseline exists
from the easier rings.
