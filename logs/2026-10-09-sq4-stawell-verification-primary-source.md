# SQ-4 — Stawell's row resolved at primary-source tier, and a real Hempl/Stawell cross-reference confirmed

**Trigger:** the catalog's own standing next-verification-target list named Stawell as the next reasonable
pick after Hempl's row was resolved last cycle, on the same "real peer-reviewed 1911 venue" logic.

## What's already known / not done yet

Already known: F.M. Stawell published a Homeric-Greek, syllabic reading in *The Burlington Magazine for
Connoisseurs* 19(97) (April 1911), pp. 23–38, reading side B first. An earlier pass flagged, but could
not corroborate on direct Wikipedia fetch, a secondary-source claim that Stawell "insisted it had to be"
Homeric Greek "in response to" Hempl. Not done: any primary-source read of Stawell's own article.

## Method and why it's non-circular

Found a digitized scan of the actual April 1911 Burlington Magazine issue hosted by the Salomon Reinach
digital library (`tpsalomonreinach.mom.fr`), confirmed by its own catalogue metadata (author "Tawell,
Florence Melian" — an OCR/cataloguing misspelling of Stawell, same journal/volume/issue/pages as the
existing citation). WebFetch's markdown conversion failed on the raw PDF; the built-in browser's DOM
inspection found the actual asset URL behind the catalogue page's inert-looking "PDF" label, and the same
PDF-extraction workaround used elsewhere this project (pypdf on the recovered bytes) produced a full,
legible 40,000+ character text layer — this is a direct primary-source read, not a summary or snippet.

## Result

The scanned volume contains multiple articles; Stawell's begins mid-way through the extracted page
sequence, confirmed by its own running header ("An Interpretation of the Phaistos Disk") and byline ("BY
F. MELIAN STAWELL (NEWNHAM COLLEGE)").

**Stawell's own text explicitly engages with and critiques Hempl, in her own words — resolving the
previously-uncorroborated "rebuttal" question directly, at the strongest possible tier:**

> "Until this year no one had published any attempt at decipherment, and full credit must be given to
> Professor Hempl for being first in the field. We who follow reap the advantage of his labour, and
> personally I have no doubt that many of the sound-values he proposed are right... But the trouble with
> Hempl's interpretation was that some of the names he assumed did not fit the signs, while others could
> hardly have been common in Greek of any period; further, his reading was in anything but natural Greek;
> and finally, the sense it gave could not be called satisfactory."

This is not the "insisted it had to be" framing the earlier secondary source used — Stawell's actual
tone is collegial and credits Hempl as first in the field — but it **is** a real, specific, three-part
technical critique (names not fitting signs, unnatural Greek, unsatisfactory resulting sense), in her own
words, at primary-source tier.

**A specific sign-level cross-reference between the two 1911 papers is also directly confirmed**: Stawell
credits a correspondent, James Cunningham, as "the first to read [sign] 25 ([a value] on Hempl's system,
[a different value] on mine)" — a named, specific, sign-by-sign comparison between her reading and
Hempl's, not a vague general awareness.

**Her stated method**: she describes starting independently from the disc's own sign-shapes, then
comparing her derived values against the Cyprian syllabary (via Thumb's *Handbuch der Griechischen
Dialekte*, 1909) as a secondary check, finding correspondence in 21 of 27 comparable sounds — disclosed
here as her own claimed methodology, not independently verified by this project.

## Honesty precommitment, kept

The earlier secondary-source "insisted it had to be" framing is **not confirmed** by the primary text —
disclosed as a correction, not silently dropped. What the primary text **does** confirm, more precisely
and more strongly than the secondary source claimed, is a real, specific, named technical critique and a
real, named, sign-level cross-reference. Not reading the remaining ~14 pages of the article (the full
explanatory sign table and reading-direction argument) is an explicit, disclosed scope limit — diminishing
returns for this cycle relative to the already-substantial result obtained.

## Decision

Stawell's row is upgraded from "wholly unverified" to **primary-source-tier confirmed**, with a specific,
named critique of Hempl in her own words. This also strengthens the Hempl row (added last cycle via
Gleye 1912) with a second, independent, contemporaneous critique — from a rival decipherer working the
same material in the same year, not just a later specialist. Two of the catalog's three remaining
wholly-unverified rows are now resolved (Georgiev, Lozano still lack this layer).
