## What's already known / not done yet

Already known: a matched pair exists (Gsimonov, both CC0, 30 January 2024) per ChatGPT's independent

## Result

Both original files fetched directly from Wikimedia's upload servers (not the wiki page, the actual
binary):

- **Side A**: `https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/9/9d/Phaistos_Disc_%E2%80%94_Side_A.jpg`,
  4,818 × 3,216 px, SHA256 `e83e525ecfff0b7961a94e159e070c20a51cdda482a9ec01544e2701e74214aa`.
- **Side B**: `https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/3/3d/Phaistos_Disc_%E2%80%94_Side_B.jpg`,
  5,118 × 3,417 px, SHA256 `ae17e24687a64c205ffbd90928073e78f313f55d0186639b5134748701a2d526`.

Both files' EXIF metadata confirms they genuinely are a matched pair from a single photography/processing
session: same camera (Nikon D750), same RAW-processing software and identical processing timestamp
(`2025:05:15 13:25:51`, Adobe Photoshop Camera Raw 17.3.1) — independent confirmation beyond just trusting
the Commons page text, since EXIF data isn't something a page description could fake without also editing
the binary itself.

## What this does and doesn't close

**Closes**: the "download originals, hash them" half of Meeting 16's decision — both files are now pinned
by checksum, matching ChatGPT's independently-supplied URLs exactly (same resolutions, same CC0 license,
same photographer).

**Does not close**: "then apply the frozen recount rules." A reliable visual recount of 242 stamped signs
across two high-resolution photographs is a real, careful task in its own right — attempting it hastily
within an already-long research cycle risks introducing an unreliable number into this project's own
record, which is exactly the kind of unforced error the project's falsification discipline exists to
prevent. Deferring the actual recount to a dedicated cycle, rather than rushing a low-confidence pass now.
