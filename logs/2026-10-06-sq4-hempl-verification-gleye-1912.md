# SQ-4 — Hempl's row: a real, named, technical critique found (Gleye 1912)

**Trigger:** the catalog's own standing next-verification-target list named Hempl as a reasonable pick for
adding the named-reason-for-rejection layer it still lacks (along with Stawell, Georgiev, Lozano).

## What's already known / not done yet

Already known: Hempl published "The Solving of an Ancient Riddle: The Phaestos Disk" (read before the
Philological Association of the Pacific Coast, 26 Nov 1910; reprinted in *Harper's Magazine*, Jan 1911),
proposing an Ionic Greek, pre-Homeric reading of the disc running syllabically from the periphery inward
(right to left), explicitly denying the inscription's boustrophedon character. Not done: any specific,
named scholarly critique of this claim — the catalog's own prior passes found none.

## Method and why it's non-circular

A WebSearch for Hempl-specific criticism surfaced a search result for a 1912 German-language monograph,
Arthur Gleye's *Kretische Studien I: Die westfinnische Inschrift auf dem Diskus von Phaestos* (Tomsk,
1912), hosted as a digitized primary text by the University of Tartu repository (dspace.ut.ee). WebFetch
failed on the PDF (binary/compressed, same recurring failure mode used elsewhere this window), so used the
established project workaround: recovered the raw bytes, extracted text with `pypdf`, and read the
passages directly. This is a **primary-source-tier find**, not a secondary summary — Gleye's own 1912
text, read directly, addressing Hempl's claims period-contemporaneously (Gleye's own competing
"West Finnish" decipherment theory is a separate claim, not itself endorsed or relied on here, only his
specific critique of Hempl).

## Result

Gleye's text (translated from German by this project; not an official published translation, flagged as
such) gives real, specific, technical objections to Hempl's 1911 claim — stronger and more concrete than
anything else currently in this catalog for a century-old entry:

1. **Reading-direction premise disputed**: Gleye states Hempl's method "rests—under the visible influence
   of Ed. Meyer—on the assumptions that the disc inscription is a pictographic syllabary and runs from the
   periphery to the center—i.e., is to be read from right to left—whereby its boustrophedon character is
   denied," and calls these "as it seems to me, erroneous assumptions," citing that Pernier and Evans
   (the disc's actual excavator and first scholarly publisher) had already established the opposite
   reading direction (center outward).
2. **Specific sign-value objections, cross-checked against other scripts**: Gleye disputes at least three
   of Hempl's individual syllabic-value assignments by comparison by comparison to other attested ancient
   scripts — sign "N°18" ('a carpenter's angle,' per Evans), which Hempl reads as the syllable *ra*, Gleye
   notes "stands for the sound *d*" on a Carian inscription (citing *Tituli Lyciae* 151); sign "N°10"
   ('resembles a kind of plane,' per Evans), which Hempl reads as *pi*, Gleye says alternates with a
   palatal *l*-sound on Hittite inscriptions; sign "N°45" (a possible water-symbol, per Pernier/Evans),
   which Hempl reads as *pe*, Gleye says represents a palatal *n'* sound on Hittite inscriptions.
3. **The reading-direction argument's own foundation rejected**: Gleye concludes that the parallel to
   Indic/Indian script conventions Hempl adopted (following Ed. Meyer) "therefore falls away, and with it
   also Hempl's (and also Ed. Meyer's) ingenious but untenable attempt to read the inscription from the
   periphery to the center."

## Honesty precommitment, kept

This is **not** a claim that Gleye's own competing theory (a West Finnish reading) is correct or accepted
— it isn't evaluated here at all, and should not be read as validated by virtue of critiquing Hempl. Only
Gleye's specific, named objections to Hempl's premises and sign values are relied upon. The translation
from German is this project's own and should be treated as a working translation, not an authoritative
one, pending a second check if this row is revisited.

## Decision

Hempl's row is upgraded from "no specific technical critique found yet" to a **named, primary-source-tier
critique** — Arthur Gleye (1912), disputing both Hempl's foundational reading-direction premise and at
least three specific sign-value assignments via cross-script comparison. This closes one more of the four
remaining wholly-unverified rows (Stawell, Georgiev, Lozano still lack this layer).
